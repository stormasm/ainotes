
### prometer vs comprometer

While both verbs relate to making promises or commitments, they differ significantly in meaning, grammatical structure, and intensity.

## **1\. Grammatical & Meaning Difference**

* **Prometer (To Promise)**  
  * **Meaning:** To give your word that you will do something or that something is true.  
  * **Structure:** Direct verb (prometer algo a alguien — to promise something to someone).  
  * **Focus:** The **declaration** or statement itself.  
* **Comprometerse (To Commit / To Engage / To Get Engaged)**  
  * **Meaning:** To bind or obligate yourself to a responsibility, task, cause, or person.  
  * **Structure:** Reflexive verb followed by the preposition **a** or **con** (comprometerse a \[hacer algo\] or comprometerse con \[alguien/algo\]).  
  * **Focus:** The **obligation, dedication, or formal binding agreement**.

## **2\. Key Contexts & Examples**

### **A. General Promises vs. Binding Commitments**

* **Prometer:** Expresses an intention or word given to someone.  
  * *Te **prometo** que estaré allí a las ocho.*  
    (I **promise** you I will be there at eight.)  
* **Comprometerse:** Expresses a pledge or strong obligation to follow through.  
  * *Nos **comprometimos a** entregar el proyecto el lunes.*  
    (We **committed to** delivering the project on Monday.)

### **B. Relationships & Marriage**

* **Prometer:** Not typically used on its own for romantic engagement (though *el prometido / la prometida* means "fiancé / fiancée").  
* **Comprometerse:** Directly means **to get engaged** (to be married).  
  * *Juan y María **se comprometieron** el mes pasado.*  
    (Juan and María **got engaged** last month.)

## **Summary Comparison**

| Verb | English Equivalent | Common Structure | Key Focus |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Prometer** | To promise | Prometer \+ \[verb / noun\] | Giving your word |
| **Comprometerse** | To commit / To get engaged | Comprometerse a \+ \[verb\] / con \+ \[person/cause\] | Personal obligation or formal commitment |
